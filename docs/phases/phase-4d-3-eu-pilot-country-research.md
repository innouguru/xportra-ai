## Recommendation

**Research recommendation: the Netherlands.**

The Netherlands is the strongest research candidate in this bounded
comparison because its official food-safety authority, the Netherlands Food
and Consumer Product Safety Authority (NVWA), provides readable, relevant
guidance for imports of food and feed of non-animal origin. The guidance
explicitly covers seeds among the product types it controls, describes
controls at border control posts in cooperation with Dutch Customs, explains
that EU checks depend on the third country and commodity code, and links to
the current EU measure it says applies. Separate NVWA plant-product guidance
describes Dutch pre-notification and inspection procedures.

The product owner subsequently agreed with the Netherlands direction for
planning, as recorded in the [Phase 4D.2 decision record](./phase-4d-2-sesame-eu-pilot-product-owner-decisions.md).
That agreement does not determine whether the Netherlands is the Member
State of first EU entry or only the import/customs-clearance destination.
The planning boundary is consignments intended for Dutch import and customs
clearance. The applicable border control post, customs route, exact product
classification, plant-health conditions, and shipment-specific requirements
remain to be confirmed. No source reviewed establishes a sesame-specific
Dutch requirement beyond the applicable EU rules.

The recommendation is based on the availability and clarity of official
sources, not market size, popularity, or a claim that Dutch substantive law
differs from EU law. It is a proposal for a manageable research pilot, not a
definitive determination of where any particular shipment should enter.

## EU-wide law versus Member State information

Substantive food-safety and official-control requirements discussed here
derive from EU law and apply across Member States. Selecting the Netherlands
does not create a separate sesame rule and does not alter the EU legal text.
The national sources are relevant to identifying competent authorities,
border-control arrangements, pre-notification channels, and official
operational guidance.

The Commission's official guidance says that EU Member States' competent
authorities enforce agri-food-chain legislation and organize official
controls on their territories. Its import-controls Q&A describes the
EU-wide process for identifying higher-risk non-animal-origin foods and
identifies Regulation 2019/1793 as the measure listing third-country
products, hazards, check frequencies, and sampling requirements. TRACES is
the Commission platform for official documents and competent-authority
monitoring. These sources explain the EU framework; they do not identify
the relevant Dutch BCP or decide this shipment's national route.

### Sesame legal baseline

The prior Phase 4D.1 legal review read the authentic Official Journal text
for Regulation (EU) 2019/1793 and the relevant subsequent acts. For the
reviewed amendment chain:

- Regulation (EU) 2025/1441, Regulation (EU) 2026/194, and Commission
  Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1206 list Nigerian food-use *Sesamum*
  seeds in Annex II, point 1, row 12. The row specifies CN `1207 40 90`
  and designated processed-product `ex` codes with TARIC subdivision `49`,
  identifies *Salmonella*, and states a 50% identity-and-physical-check
  frequency.
- Article 8(2) of Regulation 2019/1793 describes the listed frequency as
  an overall frequency for all products in that Annex II entry. It is not a
  prevalence rate, an inspection guarantee for each shipment, or a
  separate rate for every code.
- The reviewed row's footnote points to Annex III, point 1(a). Articles
  10(1) and 10(2)(d) address sampling and analysis results and the
  Salmonella absence criterion in 25 g. Article 11 contains the general
  official-certificate rule for Annex II consignments; Article 4 addresses
  release following presentation of a duly finalized CHED.
- The two identified 2025 corrigenda and the reviewed 2026 amendments did
  not change the Nigerian sesame row, according to the source-by-source
  chronology in Phase 4D.1. The bounded official-metadata search recorded
  there found no later direct amendment to Regulation 2019/1793 through
  2026-10-20. That search is not exhaustive of all EU food, plant-health,
  customs, emergency, or shipment-date measures.

These are statements about the reviewed authentic legal texts, not a
determination that the proposed raw product falls within a listed tariff
line or that every control applies to a particular shipment. The 30 June
2026 consolidated text is a documentation text without legal effect; the
Phase 4D.1 record attributes the findings to the identified Official
Journal acts, not solely to that consolidation.

## Scope and applicability questions still open

1. **Product use:** Product-owner direction is food-use sesame; confirm that the
   shipment is not seed for sowing and document how that intended use is
   established.
2. **Presentation:** Raw/unprocessed is proposed, not yet legally classified.
   Confirm the precise product condition, including whether cleaned, hulled,
   treated, or otherwise prepared.
3. **CN/TARIC:** A customs-classification reviewer must confirm the
   classification for that exact presentation and intended use. A product
   name or annex description is not a tariff ruling.
4. **Meaning of destination:** The product owner must specify whether the
   Netherlands is the first EU point of entry, the place of import
   clearance, the intended market, or all of these. Identify an appropriate
   designated border control post for the actual route and commodity.
5. **EU and Dutch procedures:** Verify all EU-wide official-control and
   plant-health rules plus any Dutch operational steps for the defined
   product and entry point. Do not infer different substantive food law from
   the existence of national websites.
6. **Nigerian export and phytosanitary procedures:** NEPC pages remain
   unreadable in the Phase 4D.1 research. The NAQS Plant Export Guidelines
   PDF remains unreadable, and no sesame-specific NAQS procedure has been
   verified. The accessible general NAQS quarantine guidance is not enough
   to establish shipment applicability.
7. **Kano and Jigawa:** These remain proposed sourcing regions only. No
   reviewed source demonstrates that either region changes the EU or
   national import rules.
8. **Buyer/importer conditions:** No buyer-specific or importer-specific
   conditions were investigated as legal requirements. Keep these separate
   from official regulatory requirements.

This is not a complete export checklist. No candidate requirement is
approved for exporter publication, and Xportra must not imply government
approval, certification, or export clearance.

## Product-owner direction and remaining approval

The product owner agreed with the Netherlands-based pilot direction for
planning. The separate Phase 4D.2 decision record defines the agreed
destination boundary as consignments intended for Dutch import and customs
clearance; it does not require Dutch first EU entry or identify a BCP.
Raw/unprocessed food-use sesame and Kano/Jigawa operational sourcing are
also recorded there.

The planning direction does not establish classification, shipment
applicability, current legal coverage, or exporter publication. It does not
accept ADR-0017 or authorize implementation. Qualified review of the exact
entry route/BCP, current EU and Dutch measures, plant-health applicability,
and readable current NEPC/NAQS procedures remains necessary before any
candidate can pass Stage B or be published.
